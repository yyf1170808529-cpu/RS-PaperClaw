### CmIVTP: Cross-Modal Interaction-Based Vessel Trajectory Prediction for Maritime Intelligence

Maritime intelligent transportation systems (MITS) are essential for ensuring navigation safety and efficiency in busy waterways. However, accurate vessel trajectory prediction remains challenging due to the limitations of single-source data. Automatic identification system (AIS) data is often sparse or unavailable for small vessels, while closed-circuit television (CCTV) data alone cannot fully capture dynamic vessel behavior. To mitigate these challenges, we propose a cross-modal interaction-based vessel trajectory prediction (named CmIVTP) framework to model the intricate interactions between vessel dynamics and environmental constraints. Specifically, we introduce a target-aware scene encoder to extract scene semantic features, effectively capturing vessel-environment interactions and enhancing trajectory prediction accuracy. In addition, we propose a cross-modal interaction transformer, which integrates AIS-derived motion features, CCTV-based environmental features, and scene representations. It leverages cross-modal attention mechanisms to simultaneously capture intra-modal semantics and inter-modal interactions, ensuring dynamically consistent and environmentally feasible predictions. Furthermore, we construct a vessel group trajectory bank by clustering historical AIS trajectories into representative motion patterns, providing an efficient and scalable approach for candidate trajectory generation. Additionally, we introduce the maritime multimodal dataset plus (named Maritime-MmD$^+$), a large-scale dataset that synchronizes AIS data and CCTV video data, providing robust support for multimodal trajectory prediction research. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CmIVTP achieves better performance on multimodal-driven vessel trajectory prediction benchmarks. The code resources for this work can be available at https://github.com/LouisYxLu/CmIVTP.

Camera--LiDAR perception on mobile robots and intelligent vehicles depends on reliable sensor geometry throughout deployment. Installation variations create uncertainty in the initial extrinsics, while changing observation quality makes frame-wise estimates fluctuate during operation. This paper presents CalibSwitch, an observation-driven framework that separates absolute extrinsic recovery from selective state maintenance. A cross-modal absolute extrinsic generator (CAEG) combines projection-free bidirectional feature interaction with geometry-regularized learning to recover complete SE(3) candidates from image--point observations and coarse view context, without a supplied numerical initial transform. On nuScenes, CAEG achieves mean translation and rotation errors of 0.248 m and 5.884$^\circ$. Under sustained geometric transitions, SRCP reaches a mean composite error of 1.46 with 9.70\% candidate commitments, outperforming quality-based and temporal-consistency gating at comparable commitment rates. Relative to unconditional per-frame updating, it improves frozen-detector mAP and NDS by 1.43 and 0.92 points. Paired geometry--observation uncertainty tests, two real-vehicle platforms, and 13.5-FPS single-camera Jetson execution demonstrate the system value of selective geometric-state maintenance for continuous perception.

Cooperative perception improves autonomous driving by sharing complementary observations among vehicles and roadside infrastructure for 3D object detection. Sparse Feature Encoding first encodes communication as randomly mask-sampled foreground features transmitted by collaborating agents, from which the ego vehicle reconstructs dense semantic representations. Multi-object tracking (MOT) in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) imagery is challenging because vehicles are small, visually similar, and frequently occluded, which leads to identity switches (IDSW). Appearance-based trackers such as DeepSORT rely on a standalone re-identification (ReID) network, whose per-target inference limits runtime speed, while the appearance of small or occluded vehicles remains unreliable. This paper proposes a detector-integrated multi-cue tracking framework. Lightweight projection heads are attached to the neck of the YOLOv8n detector to extract target embeddings through ROIAlign, producing detections and embeddings in a single forward pass. Computer vision models learn hand action only from its visual consequences, without access to the underlying embodied motor signals. We address this absence with MotorSense, the first large-scale paired egocentric video and surface electromyography (EMG) dataset. MotorSense pairs bimanual EMG bands with egocentric video and yields synchronized video, bimanual EMG and IMU, and audio across 100 hours of unscripted manipulation. EMG offers a powerful scalable alternative to manual annotation: it captures finger movement, hand configuration, and the forces exerted between hand and environment as a dense signal free of occlusion and labeler bias, whereas manual annotation is costly, subjective, and unreliable under occlusion. We use MotorSense to learn sensorimotor alignment, producing an organization of action that is shared across the visual and motoric spaces, through cross-modal pre-training between video and EMG. The resultant vision models are then transferred to downstream tasks that benefit from sensorimotor alignment: in this case, action recognition, 3D hand reconstruction, and 3D contact reconstruction. We further demonstrate a generative application in which EMG motor signals are used to condition a video diffusion model, yielding videos of fine-grained hand manipulation. Across four action-centric computer vision tasks and eight popular video datasets, we demonstrate that sensorimotor alignment provides a stronger foundation for visual representation learning than conventional self-supervised learning methods over video, outperforming every baseline across all tasks and datasets evaluated.

Concept erasure is a prominent approach to achieving fairness in machine learning through the removal of sensitive attributes from learned representations. Prior concept erasure methods typically define debiasing indirectly through the failure of a chosen adversary, probe, or fairness penalty, which makes the target of erasure dependent on a particular decoder family or optimization setup. We introduce KINDER, which directly removes sensitive-attribute information from intermediate representations with respect to a chosen family of prototype functionals, and is compatible with any deep model containing an intermediate feature space, independent of the architecture, task type, or training paradigm. KINDER operates in a random Fourier feature space, where it estimates a sensitive subspace from protected group prototypes and projects representations onto its orthogonal complement. Thus, it forms an explicit representation cleaning mechanism distinct from prior fairness control studies. We conduct extensive experiments across diverse settings and show the effectiveness of KINDER on (i) supervised and self-supervised settings, (ii) classification, regression, and image segmentation tasks, and (iii) diverse data modalities, including visual, textual, and tabular data.
