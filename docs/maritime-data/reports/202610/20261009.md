Title: MarVAIS: A Dataset of Paired Video and AIS Telemetry for Coastal Maritime Monitoring under Adverse Weather Conditions Abstract: This dataset provides synchronized monocular video recordings and Automatic Identification System (AIS) telemetry designed to support maritime domain awareness and object detection research. It is specifically built for the development, validation, and benchmarking of computer vision models (such as YOLO architectures), digital twins, and hybrid tracking systems aimed at identifying collaborative and non-collaborative vessels in coastal regions. Dataset Contents: Due to the large file sizes, the physical dataset is securely hosted on an external cloud (Google Drive). The dataset consists of: Video Data: 40 minutes of continuous footage, divided into 4 individual video sequences of 10 minutes each. All videos are recorded in Full HD (1920x1080 pixels) at 30 frames per second (FPS), yielding approximately 72,000 frames. Each sequence represents distinct environmental and lighting challenges to test model robustness: Clear Weather: Provides high-visibility baselines for accurate bounding box regression and classification. Fog: Challenges contour detection and tracking algorithms due to atmospheric scattering. Rain: Introduces visual artifacts, sensor noise, and partial occlusion of distant targets. Late Afternoon: Tests the impact of fading illumination, shadows, and color space degradation. Synchronized AIS Telemetry: 4 tabular CSV files containing the raw AIS messages corresponding to each video. The timestamps have been converted to a relative video time format (00:00:00) to facilitate immediate synchronization for sensor fusion algorithms. The data includes MMSI, Latitude, Longitude, and Distance. ### Beyond a Single Crop: Contextualized Embeddings for Player Re-Identification

作者：Ge Luo, Changhuo Yang, Weisi Fan, Rui Yang, Shaokai Wang

Person re-identification is typically addressed by learning identity-discriminative embeddings from individual person crops. However, player re-identification in broadcast sports videos presents a unique challenge: players from the same team often share similar appearances, while video sequences provide repeated observations of the same players under different viewpoints and surrounding contexts. Existing crop-based approaches largely ignore this contextual information, which can be critical for resolving visually similar identities. Here, we propose ContextReID, a contextualized embedding framework that represents a player by jointly considering other players in the same frame and observations from different frames of the broadcast sequence. By incorporating both co-occurring players and cross-frame observations, the model learns identity features relative to the surrounding visual context rather than relying only on isolated appearance cues. We implement this framework with a vision-language model that takes sets of player crops as input and generates context-aware embeddings for individual players. A combined contextual learning and retrieval objective encourages the model to capture cross-frame identity relationships while maintaining discriminative embeddings for player matching. Experiments across three multi-object tracking benchmarks show that ContextReID improves player retrieval over conventional single-crop embedding approaches in both Rank-1 and mAP. These results highlight the utility of spatial and temporal context for reliable player identification in broadcast sports videos.

### JMT-YOLO: Multi-task Domain Adaptation via Segmentation-Guided Consistency Learning

[原文](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12901) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12901)
作者：Zhenhua Yu, Jiahao Zhang, Wenchao Zhang, Ou Ye, Hengxiang Zhao

专题 B: 摘要:object detection, 摘要:semantic segmentation, 摘要:generalization, 标题:adaptation, 标题:domain, 摘要:occlusion, 标题:multi-task, 摘要:feature, 标题:learning；独立概念组=2，分数=14

Domain adaptive object detection (DAOD) aims to reduce performance degradation due to distribution shifts between training (source) and testing (target) datasets. However, owing to insufficient modeling of fine-grained features and limited instance-level alignment, existing approaches become less effective under complex environmental conditions, such as extreme weather conditions and object occlusion. We overcome these challenges by proposing JMT-YOLO, a novel joint multi-task learning framework for DAOD. JMT-YOLO is based on the Mean Teacher architecture. It introduces an auxiliary semantic segmentation task and a masking mechanism to enhance the modeling of foreground-background semantic boundaries in the target domain. Specifically, fine-grained feature degradation is reduced by introducing a multiscale context-aware segmentation head equipped with a dense connection structure to effectively capture local and global contextual features. A mask-consistency loss is formulated by applying random masking operations to target images, thereby guiding the network to learn structural context and improving instance-level feature alignment. Extensive experiments on three benchmark domain adaptation tasks (Pascal VOC to Clipart1k, Cityscapes to Foggy Cityscapes, and KITTI to Cityscapes) demonstrate that JMT-YOLO achieves detection performances of 46.9%, 57.3%, and 61.0%, respectively. These results demonstrate superior performance over existing state-of-the-art and baseline methods, thereby validating the effectiveness and generalization of JMT-YOLO.
