Recent foundation models have drastically improved the generalization of monocular relative depth estimation, yet recovering accurate metric depth from their estimates for depth perception tasks remains challenging due to scale ambiguity and local metric-scale inconsistency. Existing approaches recover metric depth from sparse observations using either explicit global affine transformation algorithms or implicit data-driven depth completion networks. The former cannot accommodate spatially varying relative-to-metric relationships, while the latter are sensitive to the distribution of sparse observations and may not fully exploit generalizable relative depth priors. To simultaneously address these issues, this study extends our previous work MTD and proposes MTDv2, a hybrid metric depth perception framework that combines explicit depth metricization with data-driven metric depth refinement. The explicit depth metricization process performs segment-wise affine transformations to recover spatially varying metric scales while preserving the geometric structure of relative depth, followed by pixel-wise depth refinement that propagates metric observations along paths with minimal accumulated geometric variation. The data-driven depth refinement process subsequently extends the explicit single-path depth propagation to a learnable multi-path formulation while preserving its interpretability. Extensive experiments conducted on 16 public datasets demonstrate the robust and generalizable depth scale recovery capability of MTDv2 across diverse sources of sparse observations and different depth estimation paradigms, as well as its broad applicability to downstream 3D perception tasks. Code will be publicly available at https://mias.group/MTD upon publication.

Camera-LiDAR fusion has become a prevailing paradigm for 3D object detection in autonomous driving. However, existing fusion detectors often establish strong inter-modality dependencies by decoding object queries from tightly coupled multimodal representations. Under corrupted driving conditions, such dependencies make the detector vulnerable to unreliable modalities, where degraded observations may interfere with reliable modality-specific evidence and lead to suboptimal predictions. Moreover, modality reliability can vary across both global driving scenes and individual object queries, requiring adaptive fusion decisions at a finer granularity. To bridge this gap, we reformulate robust camera-LiDAR fusion as a scene-aware branch routing problem and propose SARFusion, a robust 3D object detector. Instead of producing detections from a single fused representation, SARFusion decouples object-query decoding into three parallel reasoning branches: a camera branch, a LiDAR branch, and a camera-LiDAR fusion branch. Guided by a Scene Reliability Prior estimated from the global driving context, SARFusion further incorporates object-level evidence to route each query to the most suitable branch. This query-wise routing strategy alleviates harmful cross-modal interference while preserving the benefits of multimodal fusion when complementary cues are trustworthy. On the nuScenes test set, SARFusion achieves strong performance with 72.5 mAP and 74.4 NDS. Extensive analyses demonstrate its robustness under challenging conditions, including sensor corruptions and environmental changes.

LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO) is widely used for real-time localization and mapping, but its reliability can degrade substantially in subterranean environments affected by smoke, dust, and other airborne obscurants, whereas radar remains comparatively robust. This article presents a one-class temporal learning framework for LiDAR degradation detection and reliability-aware localization under such conditions. A broad set of degradation-sensitive cues, including spatial, cross-modal, geometric/motion, intensity, and measurement-recency features, is evaluated to characterize changes in LiDAR sensing quality. Their temporal evolution is modelled over sliding windows using an LSTM autoencoder trained exclusively on nominal operation, enabling deviations from healthy sensor behaviour to be detected without requiring labelled degradation data. The resulting LiDAR reliability estimate governs adaptive selection between LIO and radar inertial odometry (RIO), retaining LIO during reliable sensing and transferring localization authority to RIO when degradation is detected. The framework is evaluated using real-world ground robot data collected in GNSS-denied subterranean tunnels under nominal and dense-smoke conditions. Experimental results demonstrate degradation detection, adaptive modality switching, and continued localization output under severe LiDAR degradation. A feature family ablation study further shows that a reduced feature representation can improve degradation-aware localization performance in the evaluated dense-smoke scenarios, highlighting the importance of feature parsimony in one-class sensor degradation detection. Robotic platforms combine multi-modal sensors to operate across diverse conditions, but most dense correspondence methods are limited to RGB-RGB or a small set of modality pairs. Training a model for each modality pair scales poorly with the number of sensors and forgoes potential positive transfer across modality pairs. We introduce TartanMatch, a unified model for dense correspondence across all 25 ordered pairs among RGB, event, thermal, depth, and projected LiDAR. TartanMatch adapts an RGB-pretrained correspondence model to all modality pairs within one shared network using lightweight modality adaptation and a mixture of real and synthetic data. On zero-shot real-world benchmarks, TartanMatch reduces Endpoint Error (EPE) over the strongest baselines by 61.9% for cross-modal matching and 49.6% for same-modal matching. Low-light UAV-based RGB-infrared oriented small-vehicle detection is important for nighttime traffic monitoring, emergency response, and urban inspection. Illumination variations, headlight glare, local shadows, and thermal-response degradation cause spatially varying modality reliability, while the small visual extent of vehicles further weakens boundaries, orientation cues, and thermal responses. Accordingly, selecting trustworthy observations based on local modality reliability while further exploiting complementary discriminative information in regions with ambiguous modality preference is key to constructing effective multimodal representations. Based on this insight, we propose ReDiffNet, a reliability-conditioned differential representation network in which modality reliability guides both evidence selection and complementary recovery. Specifically, degradation-aware reliability learning estimates relative spatial reliability, uncertainty-guided differential recovery exploits cross-modal differences to recover complementary cues in ambiguous regions, and reliability-conditioned reconstruction integrates retained and recovered evidence into a unified representation.
