### CmIVTP: Cross-Modal Interaction-Based Vessel Trajectory Prediction for Maritime Intelligence

Maritime intelligent transportation systems (MITS) are essential for ensuring navigation safety and efficiency in busy waterways. However, accurate vessel trajectory prediction remains challenging due to the limitations of single-source data. Automatic identification system (AIS) data is often sparse or unavailable for small vessels, while closed-circuit television (CCTV) data alone cannot fully capture dynamic vessel behavior. To mitigate these challenges, we propose a cross-modal interaction-based vessel trajectory prediction (named CmIVTP) framework to model the intricate interactions between vessel dynamics and environmental constraints. Specifically, we introduce a target-aware scene encoder to extract scene semantic features, effectively capturing vessel-environment interactions and enhancing trajectory prediction accuracy. In addition, we propose a cross-modal interaction transformer, which integrates AIS-derived motion features, CCTV-based environmental features, and scene representations. It leverages cross-modal attention mechanisms to simultaneously capture intra-modal semantics and inter-modal interactions, ensuring dynamically consistent and environmentally feasible predictions. Furthermore, we construct a vessel group trajectory bank by clustering historical AIS trajectories into representative motion patterns, providing an efficient and scalable approach for candidate trajectory generation. Additionally, we introduce the maritime multimodal dataset plus (named Maritime-MmD$^+$), a large-scale dataset that synchronizes AIS data and CCTV video data, providing robust support for multimodal trajectory prediction research. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CmIVTP achieves better performance on multimodal-driven vessel trajectory prediction benchmarks. The code resources for this work can be available at https://github.com/LouisYxLu/CmIVTP.

Real-time cooperative perception (CP) in multi-agent traffic environments is highly resource-intensive, which posing scalability challenges for large-scale deployment. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are well-suited for complex reasoning in dynamic and uncertain environments, making them a promising tool for this selective CP task. This study proposes a VLM-driven agentic framework for blind spot reasoning and dialogue-based CP. A mixed-model architecture is designed that integrates conventional object detection for stable perception, vision-language reasoning for identifying impactful occlusions, and a physics-based geometric module to align reasoning with real-world visibility constraints. This work provides a scalable, interpretable, and bandwidth-efficient alternative to conventional CP pipelines, and offers new insights into agentic communication design for future connected traffic systems.

### CalibSwitch: Observation-Driven Camera-LiDAR Extrinsic Recovery and Selective Maintenance for Multimodal Perception

Camera--LiDAR perception on mobile robots and intelligent vehicles depends on reliable sensor geometry throughout deployment. Installation variations create uncertainty in the initial extrinsics, while changing observation quality makes frame-wise estimates fluctuate during operation. This paper presents CalibSwitch, an observation-driven framework that separates absolute extrinsic recovery from selective state maintenance. A cross-modal absolute extrinsic generator (CAEG) combines projection-free bidirectional feature interaction with geometry-regularized learning to recover complete SE(3) candidates from image--point observations and coarse view context, without a supplied numerical initial transform. A selective recurrent calibration policy (SRCP) combines observation quality and temporal history to determine when a candidate should replace the working geometry consumed by a frozen multimodal detector. On nuScenes, CAEG achieves mean translation and rotation errors of 0.248 m and 5.884$^\circ$. Under sustained geometric transitions, SRCP reaches a mean composite error of 1.46 with 9.70\% candidate commitments, outperforming quality-based and temporal-consistency gating at comparable commitment rates. Relative to unconditional per-frame updating, it improves frozen-detector mAP and NDS by 1.43 and 0.92 points. Paired geometry--observation uncertainty tests, two real-vehicle platforms, and 13.5-FPS single-camera Jetson execution demonstrate the system value of selective geometric-state maintenance for continuous perception.

Multi-object tracking (MOT) in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) imagery is challenging because vehicles are small, visually similar, and frequently occluded, which leads to identity switches (IDSW). Appearance-based trackers such as DeepSORT rely on a standalone re-identification (ReID) network, whose per-target inference limits runtime speed, while the appearance of small or occluded vehicles remains unreliable. This paper proposes a detector-integrated multi-cue tracking framework. Lightweight projection heads are attached to the neck of the YOLOv8n detector to extract target embeddings through ROIAlign, producing detections and embeddings in a single forward pass. A multi-cue association cost then combines motion, IoU, and appearance distances with weights determined through ablation, complemented by low-score rescue and appearance-memory protection. On the UAVDT benchmark, the proposed method achieves a MOTA of 28.33, a HOTA of 31.16, and an IDF1 of 38.18 at 82.59 FPS, the best among the compared trackers in all three metrics, and reduces IDSW by 58.4\% compared with DeepSORT.

Learning reliable correspondences between images and point clouds is fundamental for 2D–3D matching. Despite recent progress in detection-free methods, existing approaches primarily optimize matching within a single model, and often struggle to maintain reliable correspondences under challenging conditions such as noisy inputs, low overlap, and ambiguous structures. In this work, we propose TeaMatch, a novel framework that introduces teachability as a criterion for cross-modal representation learning. We define teachability as the ability of a representation to be effectively recovered by weak learners under degraded inputs, reflecting its structural consistency and robustness. Our framework can be seamlessly integrated into existing coarse-to-fine matching pipelines without additional inference cost.Extensive experiments demonstrate that TeaMatch improves matching robustness and achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging 2D–3D matching benchmarks.

Audio-Visual Segmentation (AVS) aims to accurately segment sounding objects in video frames by leveraging audio-visual correspondence cues. However, it remains challenging due to the intrinsic semantic incompleteness within a single modality and the semantic gap between audio and visual representations. Traditional feature-fusion decoding approaches struggle to suppress fusion noise effectively, while recent methods that incorporate data-dependent priors often increase the complexity of modeling audio-visual correlations, leading to poor cross-domain generalization. To address these issues, we propose a novel adaptive contrastive and prototype learning framework, BYOAVP, for AVS. Specifically, we design a Self-Supervised Audio Enhancement (SSAE) module that introduces contrastive learning to adaptively align audio representations with gradient-blocked visual embeddings, thus narrowing the semantic gap between modalities. Furthermore, a Dynamic Prototype Constraint (DPC) module is developed to refine pixel-wise category perception via momentum-based prototype updating, while enhancing localization of sounding regions through cross-modal interaction. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves SOTA performance across two AVS benchmarks and six sub-tasks, exhibiting strong robustness and generalization ability.
