### Quantum–AI Topological Sensor Fusion for Autonomous Vehicles, Robotics and Intelligent Navigation

[原文](https://doi.org/10.62311/nesx/rb4jy-978-81-689487-6-1) · [PDF](https://www.nationaleducationservices.org/file-download/1754231/Download Full-Text Research Book PDF Pp 1-84V4.pdf)

Abstract This research book develops an integrated framework for quantum–AI topological sensor fusion across autonomous vehicles, mobile robotics and intelligent navigation. The central problem is treated as a safety-critical inference lifecycle: heterogeneous physical sensors produce uncertain and asynchronous evidence; statistical estimators reconcile dynamic states; topological descriptors preserve multiscale geometric structure; learning systems construct predictive representations; and quantum subroutines are evaluated as optional computational components within hybrid architectures. The book links measurement physics, calibration, Bayesian estimation, factor graphs, persistent homology, multimodal deep fusion, quantum machine learning, edge computing, digital twins, MLOps, cybersecurity and governance. Particular emphasis is placed on observability, uncertainty propagation, distribution shift, sensor degradation, evidence traceability and graceful fallback. Quantum capabilities are assessed through crossover benchmarks that include encoding, sampling, noise and classical verification costs, while topological features are judged by stability and downstream task value. Applications span road vehicles, service robots, aerial and maritime systems, off-road platforms and GNSS-challenged navigation. The resulting contribution is a research architecture that supports reproducible experimentation, transparent comparison, globally adaptable deployment and staged governance of emerging quantum–AI autonomy technologies. Keywords quantum artificial intelligence, sensor fusion, topological data analysis, persistent homology, autonomous vehicles, robotics, intelligent navigation, LiDAR, radar, camera perception, GNSS, inertial navigation, SLAM, factor graphs, Bayesian estimation, uncertainty quantification, quantum machine learning, quantum sensing, edge AI, digital twins, MLOps, functional safety, trustworthy AI, cooperative perception
